## `is_this_a_graph`

Four of five rows: **No** — retention, identity, and staleness require Python
ownership outside the graph boundary; graph state is per-run and ephemeral.
The librarian's "Yes" describes the summarization pipeline, not cross-run
reuse, and does not contradict the other four.

Folded answer: **the phases are a graph; the store is not.** The fan-out and
both LLM calls are ordinary graph work; brief identity and invalidation are a
Python node the graph calls into. `book-summary`'s `all_summaries` add-reducer
is the proof by counterexample — per-run graph state, and precisely why that
demo recomputes on a second run. The pattern doc now says this in its own
section rather than leaving a reader to discover it.

## External precedent (verified after the run)

The librarian's row was checked against the source rather than taken on
trust. CLIN-SUMM's method as published: seven fixed sections per note plus a
`Changes over Time` section from the second encounter; summaries retained in a
structured Parquet file and reused across downstream tasks; two prompts
(initial vs incremental-update, the latter reading prior summaries and adding
only novel information); a Jaccard similarity filter skipping near-identical
notes; a 50-summary sliding window; 69.86% space saving cohort-wide and
52.6 ± 31.2% per patient; clinician-rated correctness 4.69/5, completeness
4.65/5, under 4% hallucination; $250 per 100 dementia patients on a frontier
model versus $35 on an open one.

POMR (Weed, first published 1964; NEJM 1968) is the paper ancestor: problem
list plus SOAP progress notes, observations grouped by problem rather than by
date.

Two independent arrivals at one construction over the same kind of corpus —
an operator's production system and a documented framework — is the strongest
evidence in this record, and it is evidence the personas could not have
produced by reasoning about this repository alone.

Evidence grade, stated so the Judge does not have to infer it: CLIN-SUMM is a
**medRxiv preprint**, not a peer-reviewed publication, and the librarian's row
overstates it as "published, in active clinical use". The numbers above were
read from the source rather than taken from the persona. What the source
supports is a documented, quantified, independent instance — enough to refute
the "single unverifiable anecdote" premise, not enough to cite as settled
science.
