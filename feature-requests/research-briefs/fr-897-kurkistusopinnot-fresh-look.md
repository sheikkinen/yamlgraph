# Problem brief: korkeakoulujen kurkistusopinnot lack an outside-in national reading

**Prior art:** dispositioned in the FR this brief produces (closed-input
brief per FR-890 R-2).

## Problem statement

Finnish higher-education institutions offer prospective learners short ways
to encounter fields and study practices before committing to a degree choice,
but the offering is distributed across institution sites and described with
overlapping terms such as kurkistusopinnot, tutustumisopinnot, open higher
education courses, MOOCs, pathway studies, and upper-secondary cooperation.
An institution page can describe what one course promises; it cannot show the
national shape of the offer, which learners or decision moments are missed,
or whether published claims about guidance, accessibility, equality, study
choice confidence, and later progression have supporting evidence.

There is no dated, source-reconciled dossier in this repository that reads the
current Finnish offer from the prospective learner's point of view and then
challenges the category itself. Consequently a "fresh look" can collapse into
one fluent opinion, a search-engine sample of prominent institutions, or a
catalogue that repeats provider language without distinguishing observed
evidence from aspiration. The planning question is what bounded intervention
would produce a genuinely outside-in account that is useful for deciding what
kurkistusopinnot should become, while making omissions, uncertainty, and
contrary evidence visible.

## Constraints

- The population and vocabulary are not known in advance. Universities and
  universities of applied sciences may expose relevant offers under Finnish,
  Swedish, or English labels, and adjacent forms must be distinguished rather
  than silently included or excluded.
- The account must separate four evidence layers: provider description,
  learner-facing experience, participation or progression data, and
  independent evaluation or research. Absence of published evidence must not
  be rewritten as evidence of no effect.
- Every substantive finding must retain a dated source URL, institution or
  system owner, exact supporting span, and an uncertainty or abstention field.
  Counts and corpus coverage must come from collected records, not model prose.
- The analysis must cover both universities and universities of applied
  sciences and examine at least accessibility, language, geography, digital
  access, prerequisites, price, credit, guidance, equality, field coverage,
  the learner's decision moment, and any reported next step.
- The output must distinguish a national inventory from an interpretation of
  that inventory. A compact synthesis may not erase institution-level records
  or disagreement between sources.
- Public web material may cross an approved external model boundary. Personal
  learner data, unpublished institutional data, credentials, and regulated
  records may not. The first version cannot depend on gaining private data.
- Discovery, extraction, and analysis must have explicit source, item, input,
  call, and wall-clock ceilings before paid inference begins. A partial sample
  must identify itself as a sample, never a national census.
- The result is decision support, not an institution ranking, admissions
  recommendation, causal-effect claim, or automated decision about a learner.
- Existing repository instruments and prior work must be dispositioned before
  authorizing a new executable surface. Any graph artifact later authorized
  remains subject to the graph-authoring sole route.
