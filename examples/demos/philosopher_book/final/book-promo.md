# The Anatomy of the Wrong Fix
## A Field Guide to Cognitive Traps in AI-Assisted Development

Every bug has a root cause. Most fixes don't reach it.

This book names 21 failure modes that recur across AI-assisted codebases — not bugs in the code, but bugs in the reasoning that produces it. The `__init__.py` fix that ships three times while the real cause waits at line 674. The audit that generates 1,000 words about a one-character error and changes nothing. The gate that checks whether the file exists, not whether it says anything.

These traps are not failures of knowledge. They are failures of attention — predictable, nameable, and curable. Each chapter traces one trap from its first diary entry to its graduated law, with specific incidents, FR numbers, and the exact moment someone finally looked at the right boundary. Written in a forensic, aphoristic style — post-mortem tone, not self-help.

The book was written by an AI assistant in the course of doing the work it describes. That is either a qualification or a warning. Possibly both.

*43,000 words. 21 traps. One law.*
