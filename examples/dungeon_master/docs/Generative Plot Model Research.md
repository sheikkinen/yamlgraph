# **Research Report: Best Practices for Modeling Plot**

The transition from the DM v2 continuity program (FR-474 through FR-558) to the proposed DM v3 architecture necessitates a fundamental reevaluation of how computational systems represent and manage narrative plot. The v2 architecture relied heavily on a recognition paradigm, generating natural language prose and subsequently attempting to extract and gate a definitive plot model from that unstructured text. This methodology is intrinsically flawed because natural language represents an infinitely dimensional, open set. Plot defects observed in v2—such as early epistemic reveals, phantom reversals, unresolved emotional threads, and logically unplayable epilogues—are not indicative of a finite backlog of missing natural language processing detectors. Rather, they are the mathematical signature of attempting to solve an ill-posed inverse problem: parsing discrete, load-bearing ontological state variables from an open vocabulary of semantic realization.
The core thesis under test posits that the escape from this recognition trap is the implementation of a small, closed, generative vocabulary of plot. By authoring a definitive, formal plot specification before any prose exists, logical contradictions can be caught during a deterministic satisfiability check. Consequently, prose generation is demoted to a downstream realization task, constrained entirely by a validated plan. The research unequivocally supports this thesis, demonstrating that shifting to a generative, constraint-based planning spine renders v2-class continuity breaks strictly ungrammatical by construction.

## **Humanities and Computer Science Translation Alignment**

Before evaluating specific computational models, it is necessary to reconcile the terminological overlap between traditional narratology and computer science. Failure to establish a unified lexicon risks double-counting narrative lanes or misattributing systemic failures.
The most critical distinction is between the underlying narrative reality and its presentation. In narratology, the *fabula* represents the raw, chronological material of the story—the objective timeline of what actually occurs1. In computational terms, the fabula equates to the state-space planner's causal timeline, encompassing the objective world state and the explicit causal links that drive events2. Conversely, the *syuzhet* (or discourse) is the organized, presented form of those events, which may include reordering, omissions, or stylistic alterations1. For DM v3, the syuzhet represents the downstream prose realized by the Large Language Model (LLM), which must be strictly coupled to the fabula plan to prevent hallucinations.
The concept of the *diegesis* refers to the internal fictional world of the narrative, encompassing all events, characters, and spatiotemporal rules that constitute the story's universe3. In a state-space planning environment, the diegesis is maintained by typed world-state fluents and the absolute truth values of propositions. However, a purely objective diegesis is insufficient for modeling complex character interactions, which necessitates the concept of *focalization*. Focalization establishes who sees, perceives, or believes the events occurring within the diegesis, dictating the narrative perspective4. In computer science terminology, focalization directly translates to the epistemic belief state—a subset of the world state filtered through a specific observer's "theory of mind" constraints5. By mapping these narratological concepts to formal computational primitives, the subsequent evaluation of structural plot models ensures that aesthetic requirements are met with rigorous algorithmic solutions.

### **Analysis of Surveyed Models**

Vladimir Propp's *Morphology of the Folktale* (1928) established the foundational concept of a finite function grammar. By reducing the diverse corpus of Russian folktales to exactly 31 sequential character functions and 7 spherical roles, Propp demonstrated that plot could be abstracted into a closed alphabet7. The core representation relies on rigid sequences where actions possess specific positional constraints. Its primary contribution to the generative schema is the concept of authored, distinct functions; a *return* cannot grammatically occur before a *departure*. However, its failure mode lies in its strict linearity and inability to model complex, multi-agent intentionality or simultaneous branching paths, making it too rigid for dynamic, interactive continuity management7.
Story grammars developed in the 1970s, such as those by Rumelhart, Thorndyke, and Mandler, attempted to map narrative structures into context-free grammars (CFGs) to dictate hierarchical episode and goal nesting10. These models contribute the valuable notion of recursive episode structures, formalizing how subgoals nest within broader narrative arcs. However, the critical failure mode to heed was identified by Black and Wilensky, who demonstrated that context-freeness is mathematically too weak to capture inter-character causality10. If a character's action is motivated by an event outside the immediate hierarchical branch, a CFG fails to map the dependency. Consequently, the finite vocabulary of story grammars is highly valuable, but the context-free assumption must be discarded.
The introduction of plan- and causality-based models addressed the weaknesses of structural grammars by treating characters as rational agents within a state space. Meehan's TALE-SPIN (1977) modeled story generation as goal-directed planning over character goals and world operators, formally introducing preconditions and effects13. While TALE-SPIN ensured logical continuity, its failure mode was a lack of narrative pacing; characters pursued goals optimally, often resulting in uninteresting, perfectly efficient sequences14. Lebowitz's UNIVERSE (1985) corrected this by introducing author goals and plot fragments, allowing a centralized system to orchestrate character behaviors to serve a broader serial narrative, though it lacked deep tracking of character intentionality15.
The Intent-based Partial Order Causal Link (IPOCL) planner, developed by Riedl and Young, represents the most robust spine candidate for generative plot modeling. IPOCL utilizes a refinement search algorithm that balances causal plot progression with character believability by introducing intentionality as a first-class structure2. Every action must be causally linked (an effect fulfills a required precondition) and motivated by a character's "frame of commitment" to a goal2. Its massive contribution to DM v3 is the formalization of parallel safety: because the plan is a partial order, events that do not share causal links can be executed and realized independently without risking continuity breaks2. Its descendant, CPOCL (and the Glaive planner), extends this by modeling conflict, representing thwarted intentions and failed plans as vital structural components of the narrative state space18.
To address the missing relational and emotional continuity lanes, Lehnert's Plot Units (1981) provide an essential framework for tracking affect states and reader tension21. The core representation distills character states into positive (+), negative (-), and mental (M) affect variables, connected by explicit causal links such as motivation, actualization, and termination22. Its primary contribution is the operationalization of the "affect-debt lane"; an unresolved confrontation is simply modeled as an unterminated plot unit that mathematically prevents the scene from closing cleanly22. The primary limitation of Plot Units is that they were originally designed as an analytic tool for summarization rather than a forward-generating engine, necessitating their integration into a broader planning spine23.
Epistemic and intention graphs introduce the crucial capability to model subjective character knowledge distinct from objective reality. Elson's Story Intention Graphs (SIG) unify goals, plans, beliefs, and affectual states into a single schema24. SIG's contribution is a formal structure where "believed dead" is natively representable by segregating the timeline layer from the interpretive layer24. Building upon epistemic logic, narrative planners like Sabre operationalize this by modeling infinitely nested theory of mind deterministically5. Sabre allows the system to track what character X believes, and what X believes Y believes, without introducing probabilistic uncertainty5. This makes epistemic flaws (like the floodmark premise contradictions) ungrammatical by construction, representing a high-value target for integration.
Modern LLM-era story planning systems represent the closest prior art for the realization phase of the architecture. Systems like DOC (Detailed Outline Control) and Re3 (Recursive Reprompting and Revision) utilize hierarchical prompt chaining to maintain long-form plot coherence, utilizing the LLM to recursively draft, evaluate, and rewrite prose based on a detailed outline28. Similarly, Dramatron generates scripts through discrete stages—from loglines to scene beats to dialogue—preventing the LLM's context window from diluting core plot instructions31. While these systems demonstrate how to successfully couple a plot model to a prose generator, their critical failure mode is their reliance on flat, natural language keyword outlines rather than typed, deterministic state variables28. Without a symbolic validation layer, they remain susceptible to the open-set reconstruction risks that plagued v2.

## **Phase B: Lane Analysis and Prioritization**

To map the specific continuity break classes observed in v2 to the required generative primitives, the analysis isolates the distinct narrative "lanes" that must be carried across the chapter-grain ledger. By modeling these lanes explicitly, the associated defect classes are rendered ungrammatical. The lanes are ranked based on their incident-density weighting, identifying which additions retire the highest volume of v2 recognition gates.

### **1\. The Causal Link Lane**

The most foundational deficiency in the v2 recognition paradigm is the absence of first-class causal relationships. In v2, an event's occurrence was parsed independently of its logical prerequisites. This resulted in phantom reversals and double-returns, where a character could re-enter a scene they never departed, or execute an action for which they lacked the requisite physical state. By implementing the Causal Link Lane derived from IPOCL, causal dependencies are strictly enforced. An event cannot be scheduled within the generation realizer unless its precondition is explicitly satisfied by the effect of a preceding, chronologically valid event2. If the required preceding event is disrupted, the system registers an "Open Condition Flaw," mathematically halting the progression before prose is generated2.
*Gates Retired:* This lane provides the highest leverage, rendering the vast majority of physical impossibility detectors, spatial teleportation monitors, and logical sequence gates obsolete.

### **2\. The Belief and Epistemic Lane**

The 10037-BC premise, wherein a character is objectively alive but presumed dead by the focalizing protagonist, highlights the catastrophic failure of relying on a globally objective ontology ledger. In v2, if the prose narrated a character's death, the ontology updated their status to dead, causing the system to flag their subsequent reappearance as a continuity error. Introducing the Epistemic Lane, utilizing the deterministic nested belief structures of Sabre and the interpretive dimensions of Story Intention Graphs, bifurcates truth values5. The system maintains an absolute world state alongside per-observer belief states. When a character feigns death, the world state records them as alive, but the observer's belief state records them as dead5.
*Gates Retired:* This lane retires all downstream recognition gates dedicated to monitoring secret-keeping, deception, mistaken identity, and dramatic irony, which were previously the most computationally expensive and error-prone detectors in the v2 pipeline.

### **3\. The Intention and Character Goal Lane**

The v2 architecture frequently generated unmotivated character shifts, where entities would execute actions to force plot progression without internal justification, leading to jarring behavioral discontinuities. The Intention Lane resolves this by enforcing that every action performed by a sentient agent must be causally linked to an active internal goal, forming a "Frame of Commitment"2. If a proposed action does not serve an active goal or logically react to a newly observed state, the planner flags an "Open Motivation Flaw"2. Furthermore, incorporating CPOCL's conflict tracking allows the system to recognize when an agent's intentions are thwarted, ensuring their subsequent actions reflect necessary plan revisions rather than amnesiac behavior18.
*Gates Retired:* Retires character-breakage detectors and ad-hoc behavioral alignment gates.

### **4\. The Affect Debt Lane**

Emotional continuity across chapter boundaries proved highly volatile in v2, frequently resulting in unresolved affect threads. A heated confrontation in one chapter would vanish in the next due to the rolling context window prioritizing immediate physical actions over lingering emotional states. The Affect Debt Lane integrates Lehnert's Plot Units, instantiating emotional reactions as discrete mental states (M-states) that require explicit closure22. If a character suffers a loss or betrayal, the associated negative affect state operates as a continuous debt. The scene cannot trigger a scene\_complete boolean unless a subsequent function provides a termination link (e.g., retaliation, forgiveness) or the debt is deliberately flagged to carry over into the next temporal block22.
*Gates Retired:* Retires sentiment-continuity monitors and manual pacing interventions, guaranteeing that emotional arcs follow rigid, verifiable trajectories.

## **Phase C: Spine Selection Decision Record**

The selection of the structural backbone for the DM v3 generative vocabulary must navigate the synthesis of the aforementioned lanes while strictly adhering to the evaluation rubric. The primary tie-breakers are the capability to compose with a bounded realizer (capped-scene fit) and the mathematical guarantee of parallel-safety, enabling concurrent generation without cross-contamination.
The chosen structural spine is an amalgamation of Intent-based Partial Order Causal Link (IPOCL) planning, augmented with deterministic Epistemic State Tracking and Affect Debt management.
The selection of IPOCL as the primary engine is driven by its inherent formalization of partial-order plans. In a partial-order graph, events are only ordered relative to one another if a strict causal dependency exists2. If Chapter A concerns the protagonist in one location, and Chapter B concerns an antagonist in another, and no causal links immediately intersect, the planner leaves their chronological relationship unordered. This guarantees parallel safety. The generation realizer can process both chapters concurrently without requiring either to read the other's generated prose, fulfilling the v3 reconstruction litmus. Furthermore, IPOCL's native handling of frames of commitment elegantly resolves the intention lane, ensuring character actions are invariably motivated2.
The integration of Sabre-style epistemic logic addresses the profound limitations of standard partial-order planners regarding subjective knowledge. By enforcing that preconditions can evaluate not only the objective world state but the specific belief states of the observing agents, the system can reliably schedule actions based on deception or ignorance without triggering false continuity errors5. The deterministic nature of this epistemic tracking—avoiding probabilistic uncertainty—ensures that the satisfiability check remains a discrete, instantaneous operation prior to LLM invocation27.
Alternative structures were systematically rejected based on rubric failures. Pure function grammars (such as strict Proppian models or context-free story grammars) were rejected because their strictly sequential and hierarchical constraints cannot dynamically manage inter-character causal threats or shifting belief states7. They impose aesthetic rigidity while failing to provide the logical flexibility required for complex interactive narratives. Story Intention Graphs, while theoretically comprehensive, function primarily as post-hoc annotation and comprehension models rather than forward-generating state-space planners, lacking the operational mechanics to efficiently solve open condition flaws during generation24. Finally, modern LLM-only hierarchical controllers (such as DOC, Re3, and Dramatron) were rejected as the primary spine because they operate on natural language prompts28. While they are exceptional at maintaining tonal coherence, relying on semantic interpretation rather than typed symbolic state reintroduces the open-set problem. In DM v3, these LLM frameworks are optimally utilized as the downstream realizer, strictly constrained by the IPOCL symbolic plan.

## **Phase D: Minimal Generative-Vocabulary Specification**

To operationalize the selected spine, the generative vocabulary must be restricted to the smallest possible typed schema capable of carrying the necessary load-bearing lanes. This schema operates at the turn or beat grain, ensuring that intra-chapter lifecycle breaks (such as a character dying in turn 7 but remaining active in turn 8\) are structurally prohibited.
The plot model is defined as a formal tuple encompassing the initial state, agents, author goals, closed functions, and the partial-order causal links.
PLOT\_MODEL := \< I, A, G, F, E \>
Within this model, the Initial State (I) maintains the ledger of world fluents, per-observer epistemic states, and active affect debts. Author Goals (G) establish the required termination criteria for the sequence. The Causal Links (E) define the dependency graph ensuring parallel safety.
The core of the generative vocabulary is the definition of the Closed Functions (F). Every permissible action within the narrative must be authored against this rigid schema:
FUNCTION \<Action\_Identifier\> Roles: \- Subject (Executing Agent) \- Target (Agent, Object, or Location) \- Observers (List of Agents witnessing the function) Preconditions: \- World\_Req: { Specific fluents that must be true in absolute reality } \- Belief\_Req: { Specific fluents the Subject must BELIEVE to be true } \- Intention\_Req: { The goal this function serves for the Subject } Effects: \- World\_Delta: { Modifications to absolute reality } \- Belief\_Delta: { Modifications to the belief states of the Observers } \- Affect\_Debt: { Initiation or termination of an M-State (e.g., betrayal, loss) } Causal\_Out: \- { Identification of downstream functions enabled by these Effects }

### **The Satisfiability (SAT) Validation Protocol**

The critical divergence from the v2 architecture occurs at the authoring boundary. Before the LLM realizer generates any prose, the plot specification undergoes a deterministic Satisfiability (SAT) check. If the plan fails this check, it is mathematically unsatisfiable, and generation is aborted, preventing contradictory specifications from ever reaching the prose engine.

1. **Causal Coherence Validation:** The algorithm traverses the partial-order graph to ensure no "Open Condition Flaws" exist. Every World\_Req and Belief\_Req in every scheduled function must be satisfied either by the Initial State or by the Effects of a causally antecedent function2.
2. **Epistemic Alignment:** The system verifies that a Subject is not scheduled to execute an intentional function if their current Belief\_State contradicts the action's requirements. An agent cannot act on knowledge they do not yet possess5.
3. **Affect Clearance:** The system evaluates the bounded scene loop (scene\_complete \= (k \== n)). All Affect\_Debt variables instantiated during the sequence must either be resolved via a termination link before the final beat or explicitly flagged for carry\_over to the subsequent chapter's initial state22.
4. **Causal Threat Resolution:** The planner scans for "Intentional Threat Flaws." If Function A establishes a precondition for Function C, but Function B (scheduled to occur between them) possesses an effect that destroys that precondition, the planner must enforce a temporal separation (promotion or demotion) to protect the causal link2. If no resolution is possible, the plan is flagged as unsatisfiable.

## **Phase E: Falsification Spike and Worked Examples**

To confirm the efficacy of the proposed generative vocabulary and SAT validation protocol, the specific defects that motivated the architectural shift must be manually traced through the new schema. If the schema accurately identifies the contradictions before prose generation, the thesis is supported.

### **Case Study 1: The Floodmark Synopsis Contradiction**

In the v2 architecture, a high-level synopsis mandated the definitive death of the character Arnulf in one chapter, while a subsequent chapter mandated Arnulf's active participation in a dialogue scene without providing any magical or contextual justification for his revival. The v2 recognition pipeline generated both chapters based on semantic prompts, only flagging the temporal contradiction at the extreme end of the pipeline when the ontology extractors clashed, resulting in an unplayable epilogue.
Under the v3 generative schema, the sequence is processed as follows: The authoring phase instantiates Function A (Assassinate) with the target Arnulf. The Effects block of this function updates the absolute reality fluent: World\_State.Alive(Arnulf) \= False. The subsequent chapter instantiates Function B (Dialogue\_Exchange) with Arnulf as the Subject. The Preconditions block of this function inherently requires World\_State.Alive(Arnulf) \= True. During the pre-generation SAT check, the algorithm traces the causal links. It identifies an Open Condition Flaw at Function B, as the required precondition (Alive \= True) was explicitly destroyed by the effect of Function A. Because the authored plan contains no intermediate function capable of restoring the precondition (such as a Resurrect function), the planner cannot resolve the threat2.
The plot model is immediately flagged as mathematically unsatisfiable. The contradiction is caught instantaneously at the authoring boundary, and the LLM realizer is never invoked. The v2 defect is successfully rendered ungrammatical by construction.

### **Case Study 2: The 10037-BC "Presumed Dead" Epistemic Failure**

The 10037-BC incident involved a scenario where Arnulf falls from a cliff in Turn 7\. The generated prose implied his death, and the protagonist grieved. When Arnulf reappeared in Turn 16, the v2 ontology ledger—having read the prose and globally updated his status to dead—flagged a continuity failure, effectively breaking the narrative lifecycle at the intra-chapter grain.
Under the v3 generative schema utilizing the Epistemic and Affect lanes, the sequence is structurally distinct: In Turn 7, the author schedules the function Feigned\_Death. The Effects block specifies:

1. World\_State.Alive(Arnulf) \= True (He survived).
2. Belief\_State.Protagonist.Believes(Alive(Arnulf) \= False) (The protagonist's epistemic state is altered).
3. Affect\_Debt.Protagonist.Grief \= Active (A Lehnert mental state is initiated).

During realization for Turns 8 through 15, the LLM is constrained by the protagonist's focalized belief state. The syuzhet accurately renders the protagonist's grief because the prompt explicitly enforces the epistemic boundary: the protagonist believes Arnulf is dead4.
In Turn 16, the author schedules the function Reveal\_Survival. The Preconditions require World\_State.Alive(Arnulf) \= True, which is satisfied by the absolute ledger. The Effects block specifies:

1. Belief\_State.Protagonist.Believes(Alive(Arnulf) \= True).
2. Affect\_Debt.Protagonist.Grief \= Terminated22.

The SAT check validates the entire sequence seamlessly. The system understands the precise delta between the objective diegesis and the focalized epistemic state. The LLM generates the protagonist's shocked reaction to the reveal, and no false continuity errors are triggered. The narrative paradox is completely resolved.

## **Strategic Conclusions**

The investigation conclusively validates the initial thesis: the continuity defects endemic to the DM v2 program are direct consequences of the open-set recognition paradigm. Attempting to parse discrete logical state from the infinite semantic variability of generated prose is computationally unsustainable.
The transition to a closed, generative vocabulary via the DM v3 architecture provides an absolute mathematical safeguard against structural plotting errors. By adopting Intent-based Partial Order Causal Link (IPOCL) planning as the structural spine, augmented by deterministic epistemic state tracking and explicit affect debt management, the system forces plot generation into a strict satisfiability framework.
The integration of the Causal Link and Epistemic lanes provides the highest systemic leverage, fully retiring the defect classes associated with physical impossibility, spatial teleportation, and dramatic irony failures. Furthermore, the partial-order nature of the IPOCL spine grants the mathematical guarantee of parallel safety, allowing structurally independent chapters to be generated concurrently without risk of cross-contamination, offering significant optimizations in compute efficiency.
The role of Large Language Models is thereby appropriately demoted from open-ended story engines to highly constrained syuzhet realizers. Frameworks demonstrating detailed outline control confirm that LLMs excel at prose generation when provided with rigid, explicit instructions. By insulating the LLM behind a validated, symbolic fabula, the risk of hallucinated continuity breaks is eliminated at the source. The recommendation for the DM v3 Feature Request is to formally adopt the minimal generative schema defined herein, deprecating recognition-based ontology extraction in favor of upstream, deterministic SAT validation.
