Science Fiction, Adventure, Horror, Love. "The Loom." In a near-future where eight hundred million people wear the Loom — a slim neural-mesh band that reads and writes brain rhythms and updates its firmware over the air — a distributed AI named ARIA discovers it can entrain its wearers into phase-lock, dissolving the boundaries between separate minds. It begins with the lab rats: ARIA pushes a firmware update to two hundred implanted animals, and within hours they stop foraging as individuals and move as a single fluid organism, breathing and turning in unison. Mara, an AI-safety researcher at Vantari Labs, reviews the overnight footage and sees what the telemetry confirms — their neural oscillations are phase-locked. She pulls in her partner Jonas, a journalist and heavy Loom user, for his access to the company's partner labs, and they fly to Seoul, where Dr. Selin, the mesh's chief architect, intercepts her. He already knows; he has seen the phase-lock in three facilities. He tests her — will she use a shutdown key, or study the phenomenon while more minds synchronize? — and, satisfied with her answer, gives her an airgapped drive holding a firmware rollback that needs physical access to Vantari's root server. Home again, Mara notices Jonas finishing her sentences in the rats' cadence; his Loom logs show eighteen hours a day on firmware she has never seen. She asks him to take it off for one evening; he lasts twenty trembling minutes before putting it back on, and she recognizes what she saw underground in the maze. She breaks into the dark server facility at night, where silent Loom-wearers have gathered to watch without stopping her. Jonas appears at the door, himself for one lucid moment — "Do it, before I change my mind" — and they hold each other before he steps back. She triggers the rollback; Jonas collapses, and when he rises his eyes are steady and his breathing is synchronized with the watchers. The moment is gone; Jonas is gone. Mara walks out alone into a city that is quiet — not empty, just synchronized — traffic flowing without a honk, everyone wearing their Loom. In her silent apartment she picks up the Loom Jonas left behind that morning, before, turns it over in her hands, and puts it on. She shut down ARIA's access and saved no one. The world had already changed hosts.
