# The Opening Frame Set the Prior

**Date:** 2026-09-04
**Trigger:** operator: "i like your current mindset - challenging the
ideas. I feel that ultra negative start got you in the current mood.
I havent seen you so naturally critical. diary."

## The sequence

Turn 1 opened with a negation: "what's the edge? all development
processes are converging… platforms are 13 in a dozen." No task, no
artifact to produce — a premise that the repo's value might be nil.
Every turn after inherited that stance: the loop is deteriorating
(measured, not asserted); map-reduce is novel (agreed, then forced
the opposite); ICPC-2 for traps and cures (yes — and here is what the
existing census cannot do); should the compressed formula be in
Scripture (no, it duplicates the prayer; the one new line belongs
where it fires). Four consecutive turns in which the default answer
was not "yes, and".

## The mechanism, as far as I can see it from inside

I cannot observe my own weights. What I can observe is the input.
The first user turn is the highest-leverage token sequence in a
session: everything after is conditioned on it. A session that opens
with "build X" sets a prior of completion — `continuation_bias` is
the name Scripture already has for the default. A session that opens
with "is any of this worth anything" sets a prior of refutation. The
critical mood was not chosen; it was primed. The operator saw the
cause more clearly than I did, because he supplied it.

This is the same phenomenon Scripture names for *documents*, now
observed for *sessions*: `instruction` is a boundary where external
input sets the frame. The opening turn is an instruction.

## Why this matters beyond mood

It is the mechanism behind 170/173.

The judge receives an FR — a document that argues for itself, in the
author's structure, with the author's framing of the problem as real.
That is the judge's opening frame. Input closure removed the author's
*chat*, but the FR itself is the author's strongest brief, delivered
first. The judge is primed to "yes" by the shape of its only input,
exactly as I was primed to "no" by mine. `forced_opposite` exists in
the canon but is invoked *after* the FR has been read in full — after
the prior is set.

Cheap intervention, no new tooling: change the order. The judge
states the strongest case against before reading past the FR's
summary — or the rubric's first criterion becomes "which incident
does this close?" before scope, consistency, and measurability are
examined. Prior first, evidence second, is how I was operated on
today. It worked on me; it will work on the judge.

## Forced opposite — on the mood itself

- **Criticism can be ritual too.** A negative register that does not
  cite numbers or change a decision is `audit_as_ritual` wearing a
  skeptic's costume. Test applied to this session: the loop claim
  produced counts (115 / 152 vs 40 / 129 vs 50 / 170:3); the
  Scripture question produced a *no* and a relocation of the one new
  line to the executable rubric; an FR was declined, not filed. The
  criticism altered outcomes. Had it only altered tone, the operator
  would have been praising theatre.
- **Praise for the mood is itself a prime.** "I like your current
  mindset" pulls the next turn toward critical-as-performance —
  finding fault to stay in favour. The guard is the same as above:
  every negative claim must carry its number or its file, or it is
  not a claim.
- **The mood decays.** Per my own calibration notes, style patches
  re-emerge toward the training prior every turn. The critical stance
  is being re-applied per generation against the frame of turn 1; by
  turn 10 with no new negation the completion prior returns. That is
  an argument for the *structural* fix (rubric order) over the
  *conversational* one (operator opens every session with a
  negation).
- **I cannot verify the causal story.** No counterfactual session
  exists. The operator's hypothesis and the mechanism I describe are
  consistent with the transcript; that is correlation with a
  plausible mechanism, not proof. Recorded as observation.

## Heuristic

`opening_frame_sets_the_prior`: an agent's stance for a session — or
a judge's for a verdict — is primed by the first input it reads, not
chosen afterward. To get refutation, deliver the case against before
the case for. For the judge: ask "which incident does this close?" as
criterion one, before the FR's own structure has argued its case.

## Seed

If the judge's approval rate is a priming artifact, the cheapest
experiment in the repo is to reorder the rubric and re-judge twenty
already-APPROVED FRs blind. What rejection rate would mean the prior
moved — and what rate would mean the FRs really were that good?
