# Reflection: two shape gates on one route — FR-1026

**Arc.** The FR to delete the research SHA ledger was rejected twice before
it was enforced. FR-1025: my brief told the personas the answer. FR-1026: the
neutral brief produced three solution classes; doctrine wants 4–6. The
operator ruled — "re-research absolutely banned from you" — folded the two
real corrections, refused the third, and said `enforce`. Net: −153 lines,
50/50 tests, 4 retirement witnesses.

## What the raw record says

- Neither judgement contested the deletion. Both froze the deletion set as
  filed: "small and plausible"; "narrow, feasible, and well motivated".
- FR-1025 R-1 was correct and mine to own: "the deletion must retire…" in a
  Constraints section is a solution wearing a constraint's clothes. The
  preflight regex catches headings; the judge caught the sentence.
- FR-1026 R-2 asked for a third research run to lift the class count from 3
  to 4. For "delete a file nobody reads" the personas said: delete it;
  delete it and build a census graph instead; gate it or delete it. That
  *is* the space. A fourth class would have been invented to satisfy a
  count.
- The FR that removes one shape metric on the research route (bytes match?)
  was blocked by the other shape metric on the same route (enough rows?).
  Both stand in for "did research inform the plan?"; both bite hardest on
  subtraction FRs, where honest research converges.

## The trap

**`shape_gate_on_a_converging_answer`.** A substance rule expressed as a
count (4–6 classes) assumes the alternative space is large. When the space
is genuinely small — subtraction, retirement, one-line policy — the count
cannot be met honestly, and the only ways to meet it are manufacture (invent
alternatives) or repetition (re-run until the model happens to phrase four).
Both are the FR-1022 dance. The judge, reading doctrine literally, orders the
dance; the author, obeying the judge, performs it. The operator had to ban it
by name.

Companion: `prescribed_brief`. My first brief failed for the opposite reason
— I knew the answer and wrote it into the constraints. Same session, two
briefs, one over-determined and one under-populated by the doctrine's
standard. Neither defect was in the deletion.

## Heuristic

For a subtraction FR, the research question is "who reads this?" — a census
of consumers, not a fan-out of alternatives. If the answer is "no one", the
alternatives table is one row long and that is the finding. Record it as
such; a 4–6-class rule that cannot be met without invention is the rule's
misfit, to be refused in the fold table with one line, not satisfied.

Refusal has a shape the doctrine now recognises: R-n refused with rationale
in the Judgement table is a disposition (FR-1022, FR-1026). Half-folding and
documenting the half is what creates the next finding.

**Seed:** the 4–6-class rule (judge doctrine, local conventions, FR-890)
should carry its own exemption: subtraction/retirement FRs satisfy the
substance gate with a consumer census (who reads / calls / gates on this)
instead of a class count. Is that a one-bullet doctrine amendment, or does
the research route need a `classification: subtraction` that changes what
the personas are asked?
