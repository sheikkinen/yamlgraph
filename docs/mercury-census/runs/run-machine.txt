  matrix: ## 1) Capabilities (C1-C5)

**C1. Cheap large-scale semantic compression**
LLMs can map long, messy, multimodal, or high-volume inputs into shorter representations: summaries, labels, embeddings-adjacent text, schemas, extracts, and canonical forms.

**C2. Flexible schema induction and transformation**
LLMs can infer structure from unstructured data and convert it into JSON, tables, event records, RDF-like triples, annotations, or task-specific fields.

**C3. Weak supervision and auto-annotation**
LLMs can generate pseudo-labels, span annotations, classifications, rationales, entity links, and relation tags at scale, bootstrapping downstream ML systems.

**C4. Content filtering, normalization, and triage**
LLMs can detect duplicates, near-duplicates, policy violations, noise, low-quality records, language variants, and inconsistencies, then route, redact, or standardize them.

**C5. Context-aware routing and enrichment**
LLMs can decide what downstream machine should consume a record, attach metadata, select tools, and enrich items with citations, confidence, provenance, or retrieval hints.

---

**S1. Latency and throughput pressure**
Machine-consumed pipelines often need high throughput, low latency, and predictable processing windows, limiting how much reasoning can happen per item.

**S2. Precision, calibration, and error tolerance**
Downstream systems may fail catastrophically on bad structure or mislabeled data; small errors can propagate through automation chains.

**S3. Cost and token-budget constraints**
Cheap map/reduce exists because full-context LLM processing is expensive; token limits, compute budgets, and per-item economics remain central.

**S4. Governance, auditability, and provenance requirements**
Machine-facing outputs often need traceability: why a label was produced, which source supported it, what model version generated it, and whether it can be reproduced.

**S5. Format rigidity and interoperability**
Downstream machines require strict schemas, stable interfaces, versioned contracts, and predictable encodings across heterogeneous pipelines.
