  matrix: ## 1. Capabilities (C1-C5)

**C1. Instant semantic understanding**
Milliseconds-latency models can interpret meaning in live text, speech-transcripts, UI events, game state, or sensor streams fast enough to intervene before the moment passes.

**C2. Continuous contextual tracking**
They can maintain a rolling interpretation of what is happening right now: intent, tone, risk, goal changes, and sequence context across ongoing interaction.

**C3. Real-time interposition**
They can act *during* the activity, not after it: nudge, autocomplete, block, rewrite, escalate, recommend, or adapt the interface in the moment.

**C4. Ultra-low-cost always-on inference**
Fraction-of-a-cent judgments make it viable to evaluate every keystroke, message, action, packet, or frame, rather than sampling sparsely.

**C5. Multimodal situational awareness**
They can fuse text, voice, motion, gameplay state, UI state, transaction data, and environmental signals into one live decision layer.

---

**S1. Attention friction**
Users are already busy; interventions must be brief, timely, and non-disruptive or they will be ignored or hated.

**S2. Trust and safety risk**
Any live intervention can be wrong at the worst possible moment, causing embarrassment, financial loss, distraction, or harm.

**S3. Latency budget**
The system must fit inside tight real-time loops where even small delays break usefulness: typing, driving, trading, gameplay, live conversation.

**S4. Privacy and surveillance sensitivity**
Continuous interpretation of live activity raises major concerns about data capture, monitoring, consent, and misuse.

**S5. Automation boundary / user control**
The system must know when to assist, when to defer, and when not to act at all; overreach destroys agency and adoption.
